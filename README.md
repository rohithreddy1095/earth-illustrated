# Earth, Illustrated

An illustrated, interactive reference for building with earth, straw, lime and bamboo: soil tests, walls, roofs, lime, sun and heat, twenty earth houses from India and around the world, and a library of every source it draws on.

**Open it:** https://rohithreddy1095.github.io/earth-illustrated/

Set your house once at the top (town, size, rooms, walls, roof, soil and the people who will live in it) and the stage-by-stage guide, the calendar against the monsoon, the costing and the figures follow it. The settings and your ticks stay in your browser. A design section starts from the day you will live there: outdoor rooms first, doors that leave room to be, and a model before the first brick. It covers the services as well: the kitchen and hearth, the bathroom, plumbing, and wiring, earthing and rooftop solar; and the doors and windows, from fixing a frame in an earth wall to how much window a room needs for its climate. The whole budget prices every part of the house, at this month's Hyderabad prices or your own quotes, and shows month by month when the money goes out. A permissions section walks through who approves a house in Telangana, from the gram panchayat to BuildNow, and gathers the Indian codes for earth, bamboo, lime and services in one place. Fire safety shows where fires start in a house of earth and thatch, how high a chimney must clear the thatch, and the rules for flues, cables and stoves. Upper floors, lofts and stairs shows how much room your roof leaves above the rooms at the pitch you drag, and fits a stair to the rules and to your rooms. Insulation compares rice husk, coir, hemp-lime and earth by the thickness each needs to do the same work. Septic tanks, dry toilets and biogas sizes a tank and soak pit by IS 2470 for your household, a two-vault dry toilet, and a biogas plant for your kitchen. Indian flat roofs shows a Madras terrace built layer by layer and a jack arch whose rise you can drag. Polished lime and finishes for wood draws India's polished plasters coat by coat and sets out what lac, linseed oil and wax do for wood. Site and landscape (v40) shows how far a windbreak slows the wind and how far a thirsty tree reaches into clay, and sizes a farm pond to the rain and the land that drains to it. Cooling a house you already have (v41) sets the measured cooling of cool roofs, membranes, false ceilings and ModRoof against the roof you have, prices them for your roof, and sets out Telangana's Cool Roof Policy. Daylight and skylights (v42) works out the daylight factor of a room of your house, window and skylight, against India's draft lighting code, and shows what a neighbour, wall colour and a skylight change. Builders, materials and training (v43) plots places in India that teach natural building, train the trades, build with earth and bamboo or make block presses by distance from your house, and closes the coverage gap list. Version 45 adds a worked example: a one-bedroom farmhouse near Suryapet, Telangana, with no cement anywhere, a room each of cob, adobe and lime-stabilised blocks, a rain tank, off-grid solar and biogas, every part priced and every step set in order.

Every number sits in the material data table with its source and a confidence rating, and the Library section lists every document behind the book, with the sections that use it. The content is awaiting review by practising builders: treat it as a guide, test your own soil, and consult a local engineer and an experienced builder for anything you will live in.

The whole site is one HTML file (`index.html`); the 3D models use three.js r128 from cdnjs.

The animations are drawn for this book from the sources cited beside each one; nothing is loaded from video sites.
